# Preserve the issue format

Use your own accepted issues as format references. Preserve their greeting,
section order, length, footer and reader promise while improving the explanation.
Write full, connected paragraphs with specific examples. A source or writer can
inform your editorial choices without supplying your sentences or experiences.

For this teaching lane, start with 2,000 to 2,800 body words. After the reading-time quote, open with
“What is up people!” and retain a TL;DR followed by two to four main sections.
Give the issue a six-to-ten-word title and a subtitle that adds a supporting
fact or story. Explain released capability, access restrictions and limitations
beside the claim they qualify.

A builder's note requires evidence of work the author actually did. Otherwise,
use a clearly labeled suggested how-to grounded in current official documentation.
Omit it when there is no useful supported exercise. Do not fill the gap with an
invented diary entry. Finish with a small experiment the reader can try.

Count headings and the reader-facing footer as body words. Exclude title,
subtitle, source notes and production notes. Record that scope with the count.

Use a dated source window and retain topic exclusions in your private issue
state. Track creator rotation when it affects variety. A search failure is a
reason to investigate another qualifying source, not silently widen the window.
Separate source publication dates from event dates and record retrieval dates.

The disclosure must describe the actual authorship. If AI wrote the sentences,
say so and identify whether it used supplied author notes or current sources.
Do not imply the human read every source or reviewed the final draft unless
that happened. A disclosure never substitutes for factual review.

## Reading time and practical lists

Start the body with an italic native pull quote: `About N minutes to read.`
For a one-minute estimate, use `About 1 minute to read.` Markdown recovery copy
uses `> *About N minutes to read.*`. Leave a blank line before the established
greeting or topic-led opening. Keep title and subtitle in their separate fields.

Calculate from the final visible body, including headings, visible prompt text
and footer, but excluding this line, title, subtitle, link destinations, list
numerals, markup and production notes. Use 250 words per minute and
`max(1, floor(words / 250 + 0.5))`. This is an approximate convention and excludes
time spent trying exercises. Recalculate after revisions. Verify the native
pull-quote treatment and italics in the reopened draft.

Use visible sequential numbers for genuine practical advice or takeaway lists.
Preserve full explanations within each item and ordinary narrative paragraphs
elsewhere. Verify the saved ordered lists rather than accepting bold paragraphs
as evidence that numbering survived.

## Teach a useful AI practice

Help the reader use AI better that week. Select the tools, developments, prompts,
healthy habits or adversarial checks that serve the central thread. Explain what
to try, why it helps, how to judge the result and its limits. A red-team answer is
a candidate objection to investigate, not proof that the original claim is false.
Keep demonstrated workflows distinct from suggested experiments.
